This is an argument, not a statute. Adam Conner at Lawfare writes that the June 2026 executive order created a voluntary framework for “secure frontier model deployment,” including government access before release and a role in choosing early recipients, and that the framework text was withheld. He argues that secrecy, plus reported pressure on particular model releases, has produced the expectation of preclearance even though the order says it does not authorize a mandatory license.

Read the essay beside the order’s sentence. One document describes a security collaboration. The other argues that the collaboration has become a lock held out of public view, including over which foreign institutions may touch a model. Acheron is the argument that leverage has moved into a room the public cannot enter. Lyra is the claim, still untestable while the framework is secret, that early access for defenders is what keeps critical systems patched. The coordinate is whether “voluntary” can be checked by anyone outside the room.
