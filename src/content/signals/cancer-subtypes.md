UNMC researchers describe ViSTA, a model that treats mutation patterns in genomic DNA as a language and predicts breast cancer subtype from that sequence. The work was published in Genome Biology. The lab says the model found subtype-specific regions that might, later, matter for how a tumor responds to drugs. The same note says the team is not making a clinical claim. ViSTA was tested on two existing cohorts and released for other researchers. It is a computational result, not a diagnosis.

Lyra is the straight reading. A public research model that makes a hidden pattern legible can shorten the path to a more precise question about treatment, if later experiments earn that step. Acheron is the misuse waiting beside it. A pattern-finder that a person treats as a verdict, before a clinician and a trial have agreed, is a ranking without a hearing. The coordinate is the distance the researchers themselves marked: between a shared model and a decision about a patient.
