In system theory, a terminal state is a condition a process can reach and then stay in. Inputs may keep arriving. The struggle that defined the path stops deciding the outcome. A close cousin in mathematics is the absorbing state: once the system is there, it does not leave.

The everyday word is smaller. A terminal is the glass in a hand, a screen waiting for the next command. The name of this site holds both meanings. The screen is the instrument. The terminal state is a claim about where the instrument points.

The claim is narrow enough to argue with. The planetary facts are shared: energy, water, medicine, labor, and the computers now threaded through all of them. The stories told about those facts divide. One story treats advanced AI as systemic triage. When the grid, the clinic, the model, and the logistics cannot all be served, something is ranked and something is switched off. The other story treats the same tools as synthetic elevation. They are aimed at scarcity itself, in materials, in medicine, and in the long maintenance of a human life.

This site calls the first reading Acheron and the second Lyra. They are lenses, not parties, and not predictions with a date attached. Acheron asks what a system will sacrifice to keep running. Lyra asks what a system can lift so a person no longer has to carry it. A news story, a paper, or a product launch can answer both questions at once. When it does, the interesting object is the shared coordinate: one arrangement of power, data, and care, seen from both sides.

The work of the site is to hold the risk and the benefit in the same hand long enough to see which one a given story is actually offering, and which one it is quietly assuming. The [Acheron paper](/acheron) is the risk reading. The [Lyra paper](/lyra) is the benefit reading. The [method](/papers/both-vectors) is how to use them on a live story. The [signals](/signals) are the stories.
